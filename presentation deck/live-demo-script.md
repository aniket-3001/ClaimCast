# ClaimCast — 3-Minute Demo (Speaker Notes)

One idea, proven twice, live. Everything else is cut. If you remember nothing
else from this document: **say less, click less, let the number do the
talking, and pause after every big number.**

## Before you start

- Dev server running (`npm run dev` in `web/`, port 5174), page reloaded.
- **Before the clock starts**: on the "Start" tab, type "Abhishek Jha" into
  both name fields, leave the policy on Health Shield Classic, leave age at
  45, click the upload box, wait for extraction, click "Confirm and
  continue." Then click "The path." You should be sitting there, header
  already reading "For Abhishek Jha," *before you say a word*. Typing on
  stage is dead air — never do it live.
- Have `policy-card.png` and `policyholder-id-card.png` printed or on a
  second screen — you hold one up, once, for three seconds. That's it.
- Know these two moves cold, until your hand moves before you think:
  **click the domestic implant** → **click "Start," type "72" into age,
  click "The path."** Nothing else needs rehearsal.

## The script (3:00)

### The hook (0:00–0:20)

*(No slide. No screen yet. Just talk.)*

> A family gets ninety seconds at a hospital admission desk to pick a room.
> Nobody there tells them their policy caps that room at half what they're
> about to pay. They find out six weeks later, in a rejection letter.
>
> We moved that letter to before the admission.

*(Beat. Now open the screen.)*

---

### The idea, once (0:20–0:30)

> This is real. Not a mockup — the actual math your insurer runs, computed
> live, right now, on this screen.

---

### Live move #1 — the engine is real (0:30–1:00)

*(You're already on "The path," already personalized. Just point.)*

> This is Abhishek Jha. Spinal fusion. Private room. Bill: ₹3,53,900.

*(Scroll to "Which implant." Click "Titanium cage, domestic make.")*

> One click. Same surgery, same outcome, a domestic implant instead of
> imported. *(pause — let the number move)* ₹25,000, back in his pocket. And
> look — *(point up at the earlier numbers, now changed)* — everything above
> it just updated too. One engine. Not four spreadsheets stapled together.

---

### Live move #2 — the one they don't know about (1:00–1:45)

> Here's what almost nobody in this room knows. There's a second system —
> government coverage — running in parallel to every private policy in
> India. Most families never check it, because nobody asks them the one
> question that unlocks it.

*(Click "Start." Click the age field, clear it, type "72.")*

> Age 72.

*(Click "The path." It lands you at the top — a green card has appeared
directly under the admission, above everything the policy does. Read its
headline out loud, don't paraphrase it — the app already wrote your best
line.)*

> "Ayushman Bharat Vay Vandana would leave ₹0 to find, not ₹1,01,900."
>
> Launched fourteen months ago. No income test. No paperwork. Age alone.
> *(pause)* He qualified the entire time. He just never had a reason to ask.

---

### Why believe it (1:45–2:15)

> This isn't a chatbot guessing at your policy. Every number you saw is
> arithmetic, cited to the clause that produced it — the same clause an
> ombudsman would cite back at you. No model touches the money. Only the
> rules do.

---

### The close (2:15–3:00)

> Every insurance app today does the same thing: it reads your policy back
> to you in plain English. That's retrieval. It's not useless — it's just
> not the job.
>
> The job is telling a family, before they sign the admission form, exactly
> what they'll owe and exactly what they didn't know they were owed. That's
> not a paragraph. That's a number.
>
> We built the thing that gets there first.

*(Stop talking. Let the last screen sit.)*

## If you're running long

Cut in this order — never past this list, never the two live moves:

1. Drop the printed ID/policy card hold-up — mention them, don't show them.
2. Shorten "why believe it" to one sentence: *"Every number is cited to a
   clause — no model touches the money."*
3. If truly desperate, cut the hook's second sentence and go straight to
   "We moved that letter to before the admission."

Never cut: the implant click, the age-72 reveal, or the final three lines of
the close. Those are the entire pitch. Everything else is context.
